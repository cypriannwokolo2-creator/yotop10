# One Question

*The idea in one page.*

Every platform asks something of you. Sign up. Follow people. Perform.

YoTop10 asks one question: **what's your top 10?**

Anyone can browse, submit a ranked list, and argue about it — no account, no login, no password. A visitor picks a topic, ranks ten items, writes a sentence defending each pick, and publishes. Other visitors vote on head-to-head debates, fire counter-lists that challenge the ranking, and drop sourced facts into the mix. The best lists rise. The weak ones sink. The arguments stay attached to the claims, in the open, where anyone can inspect them.

Think Wikipedia crossed with a social feed, organized entirely around rankings. Reddit and Hacker News proved that strangers will curate the internet for free if the game is fair. YoTop10 makes the game explicit: every page is a contest with a scoreboard.

Three properties make it different from everything adjacent to it:

1. **Zero-friction publishing.** No signup wall means the rarest thing on the internet — a first-time visitor who becomes a contributor within sixty seconds.
2. **Quality without gatekeepers at the door.** Instead of accounts, the platform uses human review: every submission passes a moderator before going live. The door is open; the stage is curated.
3. **Identity without surveillance.** Contributors are recognized by their device, not by email addresses, phone numbers, or social logins. There is nothing to leak, nothing to sell, and nothing to forget the password to.

The rest of this book explains how each of those works, what the platform has achieved so far, what it costs to run, and where it goes next.
